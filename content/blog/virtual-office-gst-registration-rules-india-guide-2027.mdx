## 1. Statutory Legality: The 2027 Legal Framework

Virtual offices are recognized and validated across three primary Indian statutes:

### A. Central Goods and Services Tax (CGST) Act, 2017 — Section 2(85)
Under **Section 2(85)**, a "place of business" includes:
> *(a) a place from where the business is ordinarily carried on, and includes a warehouse, godown, or any other place where a taxable person stores his goods, supplies or receives goods or services; or  
> (b) a place where a taxable person maintains his books of account; or  
> (c) a place where a taxable person is engaged in business through an agent, by whatever name called.*

When you partner with a compliant coworking operator like WeeSpaces, the facility acts as your designated agent and registered address where books of accounts and statutory notices can be delivered, satisfying Section 2(85) completely.

### B. Companies Act, 2013 — Section 12
Under **Section 12 of the Companies Act**, every company must possess a registered office capable of receiving and acknowledging all communications and notices. MCA Form SPICe+ (INC-32) routinely approves virtual office addresses in recognized Grade-A commercial facilities when backed by an NOC and utility bill.

### C. CGST Rules, 2017 — Rule 21 & Rule 25 (Verification Protocols)
- **Rule 21(a):** Empowers the department to cancel registration if a taxpayer does not conduct any business from the declared place of business.
- **Rule 25:** Mandates physical verification of business premises in high-risk categories or where biometric/Aadhaar verification encounters anomalies.
